The coldest place in the known universe is not deep space. It is actually inside a laboratory on planet Earth. In the frozen vacuum of space, cosmic radiation keeps everything around minus 455 degrees Fahrenheit. Even the Boomerang Nebula sits at one Kelvin above absolute zero. Yet in 2014, scientists in Italy chilled a one-ton copper cube down to just six millikelvin. That is minus 459.5 degrees Fahrenheit—colder than anywhere else in the entire cosmos.
